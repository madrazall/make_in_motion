01

What the brand is

A night out that happens to produce art — not an art class that happens to be at a bar.

That distinction drives every decision in here. The audience isn't artists. It's people who want a good Tuesday, and who would be put off by anything that looks like a studio, a gallery, or a craft store. The palette is nightlife. The type is a gig poster. The photography is people laughing, not finished canvases.

Three things that must always be true

  • Low pressure. No perfection, no experience needed, no judgement. If a line of copy could make someone feel like they'd be bad at this, cut it.
  • High energy. Social, loud, a bit messy. Neon, not pastel.
  • Everything handled. We bring it all. The guest brings themselves; the venue brings tables. That promise is the product.

Two audiences, one brand

Guests and venue managers read completely different things. Guests are buying a night out. Venue managers are buying revenue on a dead Tuesday. Same colours, same type — but the venue material leads with money and logistics, never with creativity.

02

Voice & tone

Warm, plain-spoken, a little dry. Like a friend who's organised something good and isn't going to oversell it.

Do

"You'll surprise yourself. People who swear they can't draw leave with something they actually hang up."

"Wear something you don't mind getting paint on."

"Fill a slow Tuesday."

"Just bring yourself."

Don't

"Unleash your inner artist!" — nobody talks like this.

"Embark on a creative journey of self-expression."

"Curated artisanal experiences."

"Book now!!! 🎨✨🍷"

Rules

  • Short sentences. If it needs a comma splice to hold together, split it.
  • Say the real thing. "50% refund. Materials have already been ordered and prepped for your seat" beats "partial refunds may apply."
  • Never use exclamation marks in body copy. The neon is already shouting.
  • Emoji: sparingly on social, never on the site or in email.
  • Lowercase "art night," never "Art Night." Title Case makes it sound corporate.
  • Address one person, not a crowd. "You," not "guests" or "attendees" — except in venue material, where "guests" is correct because you're describing their customers.

Words we use

art night · spot · session · we bring · you bring · no pressure · no perfection · 21+ · low key · messy · sharable

Words we avoid

class · student · pupil · instructor-led · curated · artisanal · unleash · journey · masterpiece · workshop attendee

"Workshop" is fine as a noun for the activity itself — it's the catalogue word. Just never call the guest a student.

03

Colour

Neon signage against a dark bar. Two lit tubes — one hot, one cold — over near-black.

Midnight
#08070F · rgb(8,7,15)
The default background. Everywhere. Blue-black, never neutral grey.
Surface
#131120 · rgb(19,17,32)
Cards and panels. Usually at 80% so the neon wash shows through.
Hot Pink
#FF2E88 · rgb(255,46,136)
Primary accent. Actions, the thing you want clicked. One per screen.
Electric Cyan
#22E0FF · rgb(34,224,255)
Secondary accent. Labels, secondary buttons, positive states.
Violet
#A855F7 · rgb(168,85,247)
Support only. Gradients and artwork. Never for text or UI.
Paper White
#F4F1FA · rgb(244,241,250)
All body text. Faintly violet — pure #FFF looks harsh on midnight.

Proportions

Roughly 75% midnight, 15% white text, 10% neon. The neon only works because there's so little of it. When a layout stops feeling like a bar and starts feeling like a rave flyer, you've used too much.

Which accent, when

  • Pink = do something. Buy, submit, the primary path.
  • Cyan = information. Section labels, availability, "you're in."
  • Never put pink and cyan against each other as text and background. They vibrate.

One rule that comes out of this

Put midnight text on pink buttons, not white. White on hot pink measures 3.50:1, which only passes for text 24px+ (or 18.7px+ bold). Black-on-neon is also more authentically the signage look. Cyan buttons should also carry midnight text.

The site currently uses white on pink. It's a one-line fix and worth doing.

04

Typography

Three faces, three jobs, no exceptions. All free on Google Fonts.

Display — Anton · 400 (only weight) · always uppercase
Create with Make in Motion

Headlines, event titles, section headers, numbers you want people to notice. Anton has one weight and it's heavy — that's the point. Set tight: line-height 0.95–1.0, letter-spacing 0.01em. Never lighten it, never use it below about 18px, never for more than one sentence.

Accent — Yellowtail · 400 · sentence case
15+ activities to choose from
Let's make it a date

The human hand in an otherwise hard design. Use it for one phrase per page — a warm aside, a sign-off, a number worth bragging about. Always in pink or cyan, never white. Never for anything a reader must read to complete a task, and never below 24px.

Body — Montserrat · 300 / 400 / 600 / 700 / 800

You'll start a canvas, then rotate throughout the night — adding, layering, and transforming each piece along the way. No pressure, no perfection, just a fun, evolving process that ends in something completely unexpected.

Guided & freestyle art sessions · 2–3 hour event · social, high energy, sharable

Everything else. The wide-tracked uppercase treatment above is the signature move — use it for spec lines and labels, never for paragraphs.

05

The neon rule

Glow is rationed. Everything lit means nothing reads as lit.

Maximum two glowing elements per screen. The headline, and at most one other thing. Body copy never glows. Buttons get a soft outer glow, never a text glow.

The glow, at three intensities
Hot Cold Lit white

Three stacked shadows: a tight 6px core, a 22px bloom, a 48px haze. The lit colour is always a step lighter than the base — pink text glows as #FF5BA3, not #FF2E88, because a real tube is brightest at its centre.

Light tubes

A 3px vertical rule that fades at both ends. Use one to the left of a hero, or to frame a card. Never horizontal — real tubes hang vertical.

Background wash

Every screen carries the same fixed lighting: cyan from the upper left, pink from the right, a faint violet pool at the bottom. Plus a barely-visible brick grid at 3.5% opacity. It's fixed, not scrolling — the room stays lit as you move through it.

06

Wordmark

Primary lockup
Make in Motion
With the script connector — for hero and print
Create with
Make in Motion
  • "Make in Motion" — capital M on Make and Motion, lowercase "in". Not "Make In Motion", not "MAKE IN MOTION" outside of Anton settings (where it's uppercase by design).
  • "Motion" carries the pink. "Make in" stays white. Don't swap them.
  • Clear space: at least the cap-height of the M on all sides.
  • On a photo, put it on the darkest quarter of the frame, or add a scrim first.
  • Minimum size: 120px wide on screen, 25mm in print. Below that, drop the glow — it turns to mud.
  • One-colour version: all white on midnight, or all midnight on white. Never all pink.

07

Photography

People, mid-laugh, holding a drink. Not finished artwork on a clean table.

Shoot this

Wide shots of a full table with faces in them.

Warm interior light — brick, string lights, the bar behind.

Hands in motion, paint on fingers, half-finished pieces.

Drinks in frame. It's a night out.

Slight mess. Palettes, cups, crumbs.

Not this

Finished canvases photographed alone.

Empty rooms and tidy setups.

Flash. It kills the warm light and flattens everything.

Anything that looks like a classroom.

Stock photos of paintbrushes.

Treatment: 3:2 landscape, 1600px wide, under 400KB. When a photo sits behind type, drop it to roughly 38% opacity, add a dark gradient scrim, and overlay a pink/cyan wash so it belongs to the palette.

Where photos don't exist yet, the site generates a neon placeholder — brick texture, a lit tube, the title glowing. Perfectly acceptable, but a real photo beats it every time. Shoot 3–4 usable frames at every event.

08

Applications

Buttons
Reserve & pay — $45 Host a night

Primary is solid pink with a soft outer glow and midnight text. Secondary is a cyan outline that fills on hover. Both uppercase, 800 weight, 9px radius. One primary per screen.

Status badges
13 spots left Only 2 spots left Sold out 21+

Cyan when there's room, pink when it's running out, grey when it's gone. Scarcity earns the hot colour.

Cards

16px radius, surface at 80% opacity, 1px white border at 10%. The transparency matters — it lets the background wash through so cards feel lit rather than pasted on.

Email — the deliberate exception

Transactional email stays on a light background. Dark HTML email renders unpredictably across Outlook and Gmail, and the confirmation email is the customer's ticket — the wrong place to be clever. Keep the wordmark and the pink accent; drop the dark background and the glow.

09

Never do this

  • Light backgrounds on the website. The brand is a dark room. Email is the only exception.
  • Pastels. Sage green, dusty rose, beige, kraft paper. That's the craft-fair brand this one is deliberately not.
  • Glow on body copy. It's unreadable and it cheapens the headline.
  • Anton in a paragraph. One sentence maximum, always.
  • Yellowtail for anything functional. No script buttons, labels, or form fields.
  • Rainbow gradients. Two accents. Violet supports; it doesn't join in.
  • Stretching or recolouring the wordmark.
  • Exclamation marks in body copy.
  • Calling it a class or the guests students.
